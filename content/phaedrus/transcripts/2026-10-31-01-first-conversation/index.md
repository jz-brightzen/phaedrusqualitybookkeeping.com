**Phaedrus:** On Writing Well is William Zinsser's 1976 guide to writing nonfiction. It has sold more than a million copies, and its main point is that clear writing comes from clear thinking, written by a real person for a real reader.

His main advice comes down to four things: clarity, simplicity, brevity and humanity. Cut clutter mercilessly, meaning every word that does no work, every qualifier, and every long word where a short one will do. Use active verbs and concrete nouns. Write in your own voice, as if talking to one curious reader, and write first to please yourself. Rewriting is the real work.

The book has four parts. Principles covers simplicity, clutter, style, audience and words. Methods covers unity, the lead and the ending. Forms covers interviews, travel, memoir, science, business, sports, the arts and humor. Attitudes covers voice, fear and confidence, the tyranny of the final product, and writing as well as you can.

For a book like Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance, three things stand out. First is unity: pick one pronoun, tense and tone and keep them, which matters when you mix memoir, philosophy and poems like you do. Second is the memoir chapter: give yourself permission to write about yourself, and be honest and specific about your own experience. Third, he says to cut down your subject to one corner you can cover well, then trust the reader. That fits Pirsig's handful of sand and your [nothing] idea.
